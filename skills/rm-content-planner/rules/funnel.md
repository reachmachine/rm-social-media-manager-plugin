# Funnel Principles — from Russell Brunson, "DotCom Secrets"

> **What this is:** the universal funnel ideas from the book, one per chapter, rewritten in plain
> English for building a content plan. The chapter numbers and titles (Secret #1..#20) are kept only
> so other files can cite a rule by number — the language here is plain, not hype.
> **How to read it:** for each idea you get the principle, then one line on how to use it in a Reels
> plan — which reel is **TOF** (Top of Funnel: reach cold strangers), **MOF** (Middle: build trust
> with warm followers), or **BOF** (Bottom: ask for the ONE conversion action). Swap the example
> business for the real one you are planning for.
> **Red line (all content):** never promise a guaranteed result or income ("make $10k", "guaranteed
> 100k views"). Ad and social platforms ban accounts for it. Show real, honest outcomes only.

---

## Secret #1: The Secret Formula (Who / Where / Bait / Result)
Pick the ONE person you want to reach before you pick what to say: who they are, where they already
hang out online, what free thing would pull them in, and the real result they want.
- **In a plan:** decide the who/where before any reel. TOF reels use the hook words that person
  already says (a bakery targets "no-time weeknight dinners", not "baked goods"). Lead with the
  result they want, not your product features.

## Secret #2: The Value Ladder
People rarely buy the big thing first. Offer a cheap or free first step, then a bigger next step, up
to a premium one — each step earns the next by actually helping.
- **In a plan:** the month should map to the ladder. TOF = the free step (follow / free guide). MOF
  reels prove the small paid step is worth it. BOF reels point to the next rung up.

## Secret #3: From a Ladder to a Funnel
A ladder is the list of offers; a funnel is the path that moves one person up it. Start with the free
or cheap offer and only invite people higher once they trust you.
- **In a plan:** never open a cold reel with your most expensive offer. TOF earns the follow, MOF
  earns trust, BOF makes the ask. Expect only a share of viewers to move up each step.

## Secret #4: How to Find Your Dream Customers
Don't invent an audience — find the groups, hashtags, and searches where your buyer already gathers,
and lead with something odd enough to stop their scroll.
- **In a plan:** research where the buyer lives online, then write TOF hooks around their real pain
  (a bookkeeper's client fears "a surprise tax bill"). A scroll-stopping first 1-2 seconds is
  required — good targeting still needs an attention-grabbing open.

## Secret #5: The Three Types of Traffic (Control / Don't Control / Own)
There are three kinds of reach: traffic you pay for (control), traffic you can't aim like organic and
search (don't control), and traffic you OWN — your followers and email list. Owned traffic is free to
reach again and again, so it is the real goal.
- **In a plan:** every reel's job is to turn a stranger into someone you OWN. The ONE conversion
  action for most TOF/MOF reels is "follow" or "join the email list" — rented reach that never
  becomes owned is wasted.

## Secret #6: The Attractive Character
People follow and buy from a person, not a faceless logo. Build one real, consistent human face with
a backstory tied to the product, honest flaws, and a clear point of view.
- **In a plan:** put one real person on camera across the whole plan. MOF reels tell their origin
  story and their honest opinions so followers bond with a human they recognise.

## Secret #7: The Soap Opera Sequence (a story-driven welcome)
When someone new joins, a short story sequence bonds them fast: set the stage, open with drama, reach
a stuck point that mirrors theirs, share the turning point, then make one clear ask.
- **In a plan:** run this as a 4-5 reel "new follower" arc. TOF hooks them, MOF reels tell the story
  beats, and the final reel makes ONE ask (e.g. "grab the free guide" / "book a call"). End each reel
  teasing the next so people come back.

## Secret #8: The Daily Seinfeld Sequence
After people bond, keep showing up often with short, entertaining, true stories from real life — and
tie each one back to your offer with a simple "so here's why I'm telling you this".
- **In a plan:** the steady weekly MOF reels are these — a fitness coach's real client win, a funny
  flop — each ending in one soft CTA (follow, save, DM a keyword). Entertaining content that never
  points anywhere sells nothing.

## Secret #9: Reverse-Engineering What Already Works
Before inventing your own approach, study people already succeeding with your audience. Note who they
target, their offer, their landing page, their traffic source, and their words — then model the
proven pattern before you experiment.
- **In a plan:** build a swipe file of competitor reels that clearly worked (long run-time, high
  engagement). Model the proven hook and structure for TOF reels first; test your own only after a
  steady baseline works.

## Secret #10: The Seven Phases of a Funnel
A funnel is more than a landing page — map every step from first ad to renewal, and match your message
to how "warm" the viewer is: cold (never heard of you), warm (knows you), hot (ready to buy).
- **In a plan:** tag every reel by temperature. Cold TOF reels open on the problem in plain words;
  warm MOF reels can name your method; hot BOF reels can make the direct offer. When results stall,
  fix the weak stage, don't just make more reels.

## Secret #11: The Building Blocks of a Funnel
Treat a funnel as swappable Lego blocks — a quiz, a squeeze page, a trial, a bump — that you test and
rearrange rather than rebuild from scratch. Each block moves the person one step warmer.
- **In a plan:** treat reel types as reusable blocks (hook reel, proof reel, offer reel) and swap the
  weak one instead of redesigning the month. A short 3-4 question quiz makes a strong MOF block that
  captures an email before showing the result.

## Secret #12: Frontend vs. Backend Funnels
Match how hard you sell to how warm the person is. Send cold traffic to a small, low-commitment first
step; save the bigger, longer pitch (a webinar or a call) for people who already trust you.
- **In a plan:** cold TOF reels ask only for a follow or a free step (short, simple). Warm MOF reels
  can invite to a longer thing. Hot BOF reels can send to a call or checkout. Keep one map of the
  whole path so you can spot the weakest reel.

## Secret #13: The Best Bait (a strong free or near-free offer)
Demand jumps when something is free. Give away one of the genuinely best pieces of your product as the
bait, make it specific and useful, and use it to turn strangers into leads.
- **In a plan:** the TOF giveaway (a bakery's free "5 no-oven desserts" guide) is the bait; the ONE
  action is "comment a keyword" or "join the list" to get it. Give away real value, not a boring
  scrap — the free win is what earns the next step.

## Secret #14 (Funnel #1): The Two-Step Order + Simple Offer Script
Split any sign-up into two steps: capture the email first, ask for the commitment second — so you can
follow up with people who don't finish. Be upfront about what happens next; hide nothing.
- **In a plan:** BOF reels drive to a two-step page (email, then the ask). For a low-cost offer use a
  short "who you are / what they get / why it helps / how to start" video. Right after a yes, offer
  ONE logical next step — never the same thing again, never something random.

## Secret #15 (Funnel #2): The Self-Liquidating Offer + Star, Story, Solution
A low-priced first offer (roughly $27-$97) can pay back your ad cost so growth funds itself. Sell it
with a simple three-act story: the person (Star), their struggle (Story), and the fix (Solution).
- **In a plan:** for a warm BOF video, use Star-Story-Solution — a real person, their honest struggle,
  the solution — with one big promise, real proof, and honest scarcity only. Shift blame off the
  viewer ("it's not your fault you were told to just post more").

## Secret #16 (Funnel #3): Continuity (recurring revenue)
A real business needs recurring income — charge for ongoing access to something people keep needing.
Bond with people first; don't push a cold stranger straight into a subscription.
- **In a plan:** frame the offer around a never-ending need (a bookkeeper: "your numbers change every
  month"). MOF reels build the bond; only warm BOF reels invite to the recurring offer. Track how long
  people stay, not just the first sale.

## Secret #17 (Funnel #4): The Perfect Webinar
Teach one core idea through three "aha" points, each breaking a false belief that blocks the sale, then
make one clear offer at the end — recapping the full value before you ever name a price.
- **In a plan:** a longer MOF/BOF video (or a reel series) built as one big promise + three
  belief-breakers. Give a reason to stay to the end, stack all the value first, then make ONE offer.
  Break myths like "going viral is pure luck" or "I need a big budget".

## Secret #18 (Funnel #5): The "Pay Only If It's Worth It" Class
Deliver huge value first and let people decide afterward whether it was worth paying for. Sell one
clear, time-bound promise (a "magic bullet"), over-deliver, then anchor the true worth before the ask.
- **In a plan:** the honest version of this is a free-first offer ("use it free, keep it only if it
  works"). Sell ONE concrete, time-bound win in the reel, deliver it fully, and reverse the risk in
  plain words — no fake countdowns.

## Secret #19 (Funnel #6): The Product Launch (warm audience only)
Warm up a trusting audience with a short series of value-first videos before you sell — teach in the
first ones, and hold the actual offer until the last.
- **In a plan:** run a 3-4 reel launch to your OWN followers, not cold traffic. V1 the big promise +
  myth-kill + a real example, V2 show the process, V3 picture the after-state, only the final reel
  makes the offer with one clear CTA. End each reel with a hook to the next.

## Secret #20 (Funnel #7): High-Ticket, Three-Step Application
For expensive offers, replace cold pitching with an application funnel: a case-study video, then an
application where the person argues why they're a good fit, then a follow-up that deepens trust before
any call.
- **In a plan:** BOF content for a premium offer sends to "watch this, then apply". Let the
  application filter and pre-sell. On a call, make price the last and smallest point. And the meta-rule:
  pick who you serve, ship the bait fast (don't perfect it), build one funnel at a time, then improve.

## Gate checklist (from this book)
Before a content plan is "done", confirm all of these:
1. **The month maps to a value ladder** — a free/cheap first step leading up to a bigger one, not one
   flat offer repeated.
2. **Every plan has ONE conversion action, worked backwards** — pick the single ask (follow / join
   list / DM keyword / book / buy) first, then design the reels that lead to it.
3. **Traffic you OWN is the goal** — followers and email, not rented views. Most TOF/MOF reels exist to
   turn a stranger into someone you can reach again for free.
4. **Every reel is tagged TOF / MOF / BOF** and its message matches how warm that viewer is (cold =
   speak to the problem; warm = your method; hot = the offer).
5. **One real human "face"** runs through the plan — a consistent person with a story, not a logo.
6. **Hooks and structures are modelled on what already worked** (a swipe file), before inventing new
   ones.
7. **Free bait gives a real, specific win** — the giveaway that earns the follow or the email must
   actually help.
8. **No guaranteed-result or income claims** anywhere — honest outcomes only, because platforms ban
   accounts for it.
