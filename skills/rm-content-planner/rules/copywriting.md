# Copywriting Principles — distilled from Jim Edwards, "Copywriting Secrets"

> **What this is:** a plain-English digest of the persuasion principles in *Copywriting Secrets*,
> one entry per book "Secret" (S1–S31), rewritten in our own words for content planning. No book
> passages are copied. Each entry gives the universal idea plus one line for an Instagram reel or a
> content plan. Cite a section by number (e.g. "S8", "S14").
> **On style:** hype devices (the word "secret", curiosity gaps, urgency) are **optional tools**, not
> required — and some claims are banned by ad platforms no matter how well they convert (see gate).

---

## S1: What Is Copywriting?
Any piece of content is "copy" — its job is to move the right person to take ONE specific action.
Good copy helps people buy what they already want; it feels useful, not pushy. Judge it by results
(saves, clicks, DMs, sign-ups), never by compliments.
**Reel/plan:** Give every reel exactly one job and one call to action — save it, follow, or click.

## S2: Copy Is A Learnable Skill
Nobody is born good at this. You get good by publishing, watching what happens, keeping winners,
cutting losers, and studying WHY the winners worked instead of calling it luck.
**Reel/plan:** Post, read the numbers, and repeat the hook style that performed — don't guess twice.

## S3: Without A Strong Why, People Don't Buy
People always buy for a reason. Framework — the 10 core reasons: make money, save money, save time,
avoid effort, escape pain (mental or physical), get comfort, improve health/hygiene, gain praise,
feel more loved, raise status. Tie your message to several reasons, not just one.
**Reel/plan:** For each content idea, name 2–3 of these reasons it serves (a bookkeeper's reel can
sell "save time" AND "escape tax-season stress").

## S4: Nobody Cares About You — Make It About Them
Prospects care about themselves, not you. Framework — the I/me/my/we/our audit: flag every
self-referring word; each one is a spot where you slipped into talking about yourself, and rewrite it
into "you/your". Ask "what's in it for them?" (WIIFM) for every claim.
**Reel/plan:** Rewrite the caption and on-screen text so it leads with the viewer's outcome, not "I".

## S5: Copywriting Is The Highest-Leverage Skill
Learn to write your own copy so you can ship fast and aren't stuck waiting on others. Learn it in
order: headlines first, then bullets, then calls to action, then how you frame the offer.
**Reel/plan:** Spend most of your writing time on the hook (the reel's headline) before the rest.

## S6: Headlines Are The #1 Piece Of Copy
A great headline with average copy beats great copy with a weak headline — most people read only the
headline. Aim it at ONE emotion (a fear to escape or a desire to gain) and lead with the payoff.
Optional template families: How-To, "N Ways To…", "Mistakes…", "Warning…", "My Proven Method".
**Reel/plan:** Treat the first 3 seconds / on-screen title as the headline; write it first and test
variants (e.g. "5 mistakes new bakers make with sourdough").

## S7: It's Never One Size Fits All (Traffic Temperature)
Framework — hot / warm / cold. Hot already knows you; warm knows the problem and wants a solution but
not you yet; cold only feels the problem. Match the opening to how aware the viewer already is.
**Reel/plan:** Plan separate reels per temperature — cold reels open on the problem, warm reels open
on the result, hot reels open on the specific offer or new thing.

## S8: Meet F.R.E.D. (Your Ideal Viewer)
Write to one named person, not a crowd. Frameworks — F.R.E.D. (Fears, Results, Expectations,
Desires) and PQR2 (Problems, Questions, Roadblocks, Results). Lead with what's in their head
(psychographics), then narrow the niche until you hit one real-feeling person. Sell what they WANT.
**Reel/plan:** Pick one avatar (e.g. "a fitness coach stuck at 2k followers") and speak every reel to
her fears and wants in her exact words.

## S9: The Ultimate Bullet Formula
Framework — "It [feature] so you can [benefit] which means [deeper meaning]." Never list a feature
without turning it into a benefit and then the emotional payoff. A few strong bullets beat many weak
ones; anchor each to something concrete (a number, a step, a timestamp).
**Reel/plan:** Build the reel's value points as feature→benefit→meaning lines, not a flat feature
list.

## S10: People Buy On Emotion, Justify With Logic
Framework — the "which means" technique / Meaning Ladder: Feature → Benefit → Meaning → Emotion.
Keep asking "why does that matter?" until you reach a real feeling. Lead with fear and desire, then
add logic afterwards as the justification.
**Reel/plan:** End a teaching reel on the feeling ("which means you finally stop dreading Mondays"),
then let the caption handle the practical detail.

## S11: "Good Enough" Keeps You Poor (Testing)
Framework — A/B testing: run your best version against one challenger; when the challenger wins it
becomes the new control. Change only ONE thing at a time so you know what moved the result.
**Reel/plan:** Post two reels that differ only in the hook line; keep the winning hook pattern.

## S12: Great Copy Leaves Clues (Research)
Never write cold. Framework — funnel hacking: study what already works in your market and model how
it sells (don't copy it). Read reviews and comments for your audience's exact words — the 2–4 star
reviews explain what was good AND what fell short.
**Reel/plan:** Collect real phrases from your audience's comments and turn them into hooks.

## S13: It's All About Them — In Plain Words
Make the message about the reader's outcome, in words they instantly understand. Cut jargon and big
words; write short sentences to one single person. A confused mind says no.
**Reel/plan:** Read the script aloud as if talking to one friend; if a line needs explaining, simplify
it (a bookkeeper says "keep more of what you earn", not "optimise your tax position").

## S14: No Testimonials Yet? Use Other Proof
"I have no testimonials" really means "I need proof." Framework — the Proof Elements Ladder: results
testimonial → about-you testimonial → niche/authority endorsement → supporting statistics →
trust-building quotes. Work down the ladder until you have at least one element. Never invent a
testimonial or result; add disclaimers for sensitive (money/health) claims.
**Reel/plan:** If you lack client results, ground a reel in a real statistic or a study, cited
honestly, instead of a fake before/after.

## S15: 3 Sales Formulas That Never Fail
Frameworks — PAS (Problem / Agitate / Solve), the Benefit Stack ("If you want X, Y, Z… then do
this"), and Before / After / Bridge (paint where they are, where they'll be, then the path). PAS
suits cold audiences; benefit stacks suit warm/hot; BAB works for any.
**Reel/plan:** Structure a cold reel as PAS — name the problem, make it sting a little, then show the
fix.

## S16: Pick The Right Format (VSL / Long / Short)
Framework — the video-sales-letter page order: Headline → Video → CTA button → a few curiosity
bullets → guarantee → summary of what they get → 2nd CTA → close → P.S. Default to a short video to
launch fast; use longer copy when the offer costs more or needs explaining. Test — don't assume.
**Reel/plan:** For a simple offer, a short reel with one clear CTA usually beats a long explainer.

## S17: The 13-Part Sales Letter
Framework — build any long message as 13 blocks in order: Headline package → Shocking Statement →
Define the Problem → Agitate → Present the Solution → Bullets → Credibility → Proof → Sum Up the
Offer + Price → Bonuses → Guarantee → Call to Action → P.S. Each block's only job is to pull the
reader into the next.
**Reel/plan:** For a carousel or a longer reel, use a trimmed version of this order so it flows and
never stalls.

## S18: Killer Email / Caption Teasers
Framework — teaser structure: Subject/hook → greeting → surprising statement → 2–4 curiosity bullets
→ one clear CTA → warm personal close. The teaser's ONLY job is the click; don't sell the whole offer
inside it. Keep it short and mobile-friendly.
**Reel/plan:** Write captions that tease what the viewer gets by watching/saving, with one CTA — not
a full pitch.

## S19: The Hardest Draft Is The First One
Get a rough draft down fast; editing is far easier than creating. Framework — chunk-writing: build
copy from named blocks (headline, story, bullets, proof, CTA) written one at a time, starting from
templates and swipe files rather than a blank page.
**Reel/plan:** Draft a batch of reel hooks quickly from a template, then refine the best ones later.

## S20: Make Them Thirsty (Content That Sells)
Free content creates desire; the offer tells them where to get the rest. Frameworks — the Three M's:
build content around a Myth, a Misconception, or a Mistake (mistakes are the strongest). Teach every
step up to the hard one, then let your product/offer be the easy way through it.
**Reel/plan:** Run a "3 mistakes people make with X" reel that leaves them wanting your method for the
last step.

## S21: Love Me Or Hate Me — No Money In The Middle
Build a recognisable voice and take a clear stand; trying to please everyone makes you forgettable.
Attack behaviours and methods, never people or named competitors.
**Reel/plan:** Anchor your content on one bold, consistent belief (e.g. "chasing trends after they
peak wastes your reach") so viewers know what you stand for.

## S22: The Profitable Promise
Framework — Hurdle + Prize + Timing + Eliminator. Name the action (hurdle), the concrete result
(prize), a believable timeframe (timing), and remove the biggest excuse ("even if…" / "without…").
Reframe the reason they've failed as an external cause, never their fault.
**Reel/plan:** Shape a hook as a full promise: "Get your first viral reel even if you have under 1,000
followers." (Keep it truthful — see the guaranteed-results red-line below.)

## S23: Fix Weak Copy With A Checklist
When copy underperforms, don't scrap it — diagnose it. Check: is there a real headline about the
reader's outcome? Is the offer and its timing clear? Is there one strong emotion early? Are bullets
benefit-driven? Prefer genuine fear-of-missing-out (future pacing) over fake countdowns.
**Reel/plan:** When a reel flops, first rewrite the hook before assuming the whole idea is dead.

## S24: Using Negativity Well
Mirror the viewer's real problem or frustration first, then transition to the solution — it gets you
in sync with them. Frame it as a public service that helps them dodge a mistake. Attack the
method/result, never a specific person or company by name.
**Reel/plan:** Open with a recognised pain question ("Posting daily but still no clients?"), then show
the better way — without naming a rival.

## S25: Stealth Closes (Selling Without Selling)
Framework — the Stealth (Columbo) Close: after giving real value in free content, add a soft "by the
way…" pivot to your offer, formatted like the rest of the content. Use it only in free content, never
on a hard sales page.
**Reel/plan:** End a value reel with one casual line pointing to your offer or link, not a boxed ad.

## S26: Working With Hired Writers
Outsourcing copy is never hands-off. Learn to write your own first, audition writers on small paid
tests, verify their samples, and treat anything they return as a first draft you still improve.
**Reel/plan:** If someone else scripts your reels, brief them fully on the avatar and offer, then edit
what comes back.

## S27: Get Inside The Viewer's Head
Framework — the Magic Desk: a guided-imagination exercise where you picture your ideal viewer, step
into their mind, and answer their fears, desires, and objections in their own words. Do it only after
real research, with a genuinely open mind.
**Reel/plan:** Before a content sprint, write down the viewer's hidden fear and secret dream, then
pull reel hooks straight from those words.

## S28: The One Job Of An Online Ad
Framework — the 3-Step Online Ad Formula: (1) emotional attention grabber, (2) curiosity driver,
(3) clear call to action. An ad's only job is to get the RIGHT people to stop and click and the wrong
people to scroll past. It's a numbers game — test many, kill losers fast, scale winners.
**Reel/plan:** Grab attention emotionally in the first 3 seconds, add a curiosity gap, end with one
clear CTA; plan many hook variants, not two.

## S29: You Can't Catch Fish Without A Hook
A hook is a one-sentence story whose only job is to spark curiosity — not to sell. Frameworks —
"Unlikely Character + Timing + Results" and "Result + Timing − Pain". Keep a curiosity gap you do NOT
explain in the hook itself, and keep it truthful.
**Reel/plan:** Lead a reel with a curiosity hook ("How a stay-at-home baker booked 30 orders in a
week") and reveal the "how" inside the reel.

## S30: Build Your Own Swipe File
Keep a personal collection of hooks, captions, and reels that grabbed you or that you know performed.
Save only strong examples, organise them by type, and pull structure (not wording) into new work.
**Reel/plan:** Keep a running folder of scroll-stopping hooks; start each planning session by reviewing
it.

## S31: Polish Before You Ship
Finish the draft, then polish ruthlessly. Frameworks — the Slippery Slide (remove friction between
sections), the Secondary Reading Path (headline, on-screen text, and captions must convey the gist
alone). Preview on a phone, add captions/subtitles (autoplay is muted), and get a second pair of eyes.
**Reel/plan:** Watch the reel muted with captions on a phone before posting — most viewers see it so.

## Gate checklist (from this book)
A content plan should satisfy these universal red-lines before it ships:
1. **One job per reel.** Every reel drives exactly ONE action / CTA (S1, S28).
2. **Hook speaks to the viewer's FRED, not the creator.** The first 3 seconds are about the viewer's
   fear or desire, not the brand's features (S6, S8, S13). Run the I/me/my audit (S4).
3. **Plain language.** No jargon a beginner wouldn't get; short sentences (S13).
4. **Emotion first, logic second.** Each idea ties to a real feeling and at least one of the 10 buying
   reasons (S3, S10).
5. **No fabricated proof.** No invented testimonials, results, or before/afters; sensitive
   (money/health) claims carry honest disclaimers (S14).
6. **No naming/attacking rivals.** Criticise methods and mistakes, never a named person or company
   (S21, S24).
7. **No guaranteed-result or income claims.** Ad platforms ban "guaranteed" and specific
   income/result promises — accounts get banned regardless of how well they convert. Phrase promises
   as truthful, provable possibilities or questions (S22, S31).
8. **Hype devices are optional, never required.** Curiosity gaps, "secret", and urgency are tools to
   test — use real scarcity/FOMO only when the limit is genuine, never fake countdowns (S23, S25, S31).
