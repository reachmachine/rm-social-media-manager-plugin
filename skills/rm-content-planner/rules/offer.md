# Offer Principles — Alex Hormozi, "$100M Offers"

> **What this is:** the universal offer-building ideas from Hormozi's *$100M Offers*, one section
> per book chapter. Other files in this skill cite these by chapter number (Ch 2..16).
> **How to use it here:** you are building a content plan (Reels / short-form). Every "offer" or
> "proof" style piece of content should follow these ideas. Each chapter below gives the plain
> principle plus one line on how to use it when you plan or script a reel.
> **The red line (read this first):** guaranteed-result and income claims are banned on ad
> platforms (Meta, Google, TikTok) and get accounts shut down. In content you may promise the
> **process** and paint the **dream**, but never a guaranteed outcome ("make X in Y days").

## The build order (the spine)
1. **Market first** (Ch 4) — pick a group in real pain who can pay and are easy to reach.
2. **Pricing** (Ch 3, 5) — decide to be the premium, hard-to-compare choice. Don't race to cheap.
3. **Value** (Ch 6, 8, 9, 10) — list problems, turn each into a solution, trim, stack into one bundle.
4. **Enhance** (Ch 12–16) — scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantee, naming.
5. **Execute** — get this ONE offer working before spending big on ads.

---

## Ch 2: Grand Slam Offers
Build around one offer so good the right person feels stupid saying no. The offer does the
selling, not clever tactics. Make it hard to compare to cheaper, weaker options, because the
moment people can compare, they price-shop. Lead with real value up front to earn trust.
**In a content plan:** an "offer" reel should sell one clear, hard-to-compare thing; give away
a genuinely useful tip first (e.g. a bakery sharing its exact proofing trick) so the ask lands warm.

## Ch 3: Pricing — The Commodity Problem
Being compared on price means you have become a commodity ("same thing, buy it cheaper"). Escape
by standing in a "category of one" built on a unique method, and package the value instead of
cutting price. Optimize for long-term profit per customer, not headline revenue.
**In a content plan:** show your unique method by name so you are not "just another bookkeeper";
a reel that explains *how you do it differently* fights price-shopping better than a discount reel.

## Ch 4: Finding the Right Market (A Starving Crowd)
The market beats the offer, and the offer beats your persuasion skill. Score any market on four
things: real pain, ability to pay, easy to reach, and growing. Pick ONE niche and commit; naming
the exact person you serve lets you charge far more than a generic version.
**In a content plan:** speak to one specific person, not "everyone." A fitness coach's reels for
"busy dads over 40" will out-pull generic "get fit" content, because the viewer feels seen.

## Ch 5: Charge What It's Worth
Never let the felt value drop below the price. Widen the gap by raising value, not dropping price.
Aim to be the premium option; cheap prices lower how much clients care, how much they value it,
and even their results. Most businesses should raise prices, not cut them.
**In a content plan:** proof and premium positioning reels raise felt value, which supports a
higher price; a baker filming the 3-day sourdough process makes the loaf feel worth more.

## Ch 6: The Value Equation
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of success) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).
Raise the top two, lower the bottom two. Anyone can hype the dream; the real edge is shrinking the
time and effort. People pay for certainty, so proof and track record raise "likelihood." Perception
is reality: if you do not clearly show a value driver, it counts for nothing.
**In a content plan:** every proof or offer-adjacent reel should visibly raise the dream outcome or
the belief it will work, and lower how long and how hard it looks — e.g. "here is the full result in
90 seconds, done for you." Fast and easy beats free.

## Ch 8: The Thought Process
Do not settle on the first offer idea. Generate many angles for the same problem, then judge. Look
hard at what you actually have — its parts and attributes — because changing one attribute unlocks a
new way to create value. Treat offer pieces as building blocks you can recombine.
**In a content plan:** brainstorm 10-15 ways to frame the same core value, then test the strongest
one or two as reels instead of assuming your first idea is the winner.

## Ch 9: Problems & Solutions
Sell the destination (the dream outcome), never the tool. List every problem the customer hits, in
the order they hit it, then turn each into a solution by asking "How to..." and reversing the
obstacle. One unsolved problem can kill the sale, so cover them all.
**In a content plan:** map your viewer's obstacles in order ("don't know where to start," "no time,"
"tried before and failed") and make one reel per obstacle that answers it as a clear "how to."

## Ch 10: Trim & Stack
Over-deliver until people think "I get all this for that?" Brainstorm every way to deliver value,
then trim: cut things that cost you a lot but mean little to them; keep the high-value items,
especially "one-to-many" ones that cost almost nothing to give to the next person. Stack what
survives into ONE bundle and put a value on each piece so the total dwarfs the price.
**In a content plan:** a reel that lists the stacked pieces of your offer (each named, each with a
value) makes it feel worth far more than the price; keep the list to real, high-value items only.

## Ch 12: Scarcity
Scarcity is about quantity. Cap supply to a fixed, publicly stated number and set the cap below
what you could sell so it reliably sells out. Announce sell-outs as social proof. Only use honest
scarcity — real limits, real counts — never a fake "only 3 left" that resets tomorrow.
**In a content plan:** you may show a REAL limit ("only 20 spots this month, 14 taken"); never
fabricate scarcity. A made-up limit is both dishonest and a compliance risk.

## Ch 13: Urgency
Urgency is about time — limit *when* people can buy, not how many. Use real cohort start dates,
real dated promotions, and real deadlines; most sales land in the final hours. Attach the urgency
to the promotion or price, not to a fake countdown on the product itself.
**In a content plan:** only put a deadline in a reel if it is genuine ("doors close Friday, next
group starts in a month"). Real deadlines drive action; fake ones erode trust and break ad rules.

## Ch 14: Bonuses
Break the offer into several named bonuses instead of one lump. Anchor the price to the core offer
first, then pile on bonuses to widen the value-to-price gap. Add bonuses to close rather than
discounting. Give each bonus a benefit-driven name and a real value, and make each one crush a
specific objection. Favor tools, checklists, templates, and swipe files over more "training."
**In a content plan:** a reel can reveal the offer's bonuses one at a time, each answering a common
"but what about..." objection; name each bonus by the benefit it delivers.

## Ch 15: Guarantees
Risk is usually the #1 objection, so reverse it on purpose. Write guarantees as "If you don't get X
in Y time, we will Z" — the Z gives it teeth. For low-cost, low-effort products an unconditional
refund can win; for higher-cost or higher-effort ones use a conditional guarantee tied to the actions
that lead to success ("do the work, or we keep helping free"). Name it vividly, and never use a
guarantee to hide a weak product. **Guarantees promise your process or effort — never a banned
income or results outcome.**
**In a content plan:** you may show a real, honest guarantee ("finish the steps or we keep working
with you"); never a reel that promises a specific income or guaranteed result.

## Ch 16: Naming
Name offers with the **M-A-G-I-C** formula, using 3-5 of its parts:
- **M** — a Magnetic reason why (free, giveaway, grand opening).
- **A** — Announce the avatar (the exact person it is for).
- **G** — Give a Goal (the tangible dream outcome).
- **I** — Indicate a time Interval (5-Day, 6-Week). Never pair a duration with a specific
  income/result number — that reads as a banned guarantee on ad platforms.
- **C** — Complete with a container word (Challenge, Sprint, Blueprint, System).
Write several names, test them live, and lock the winner; when response drops, refresh from the top
(creative → copy → name) before ever changing the underlying offer.
**In a content plan:** give your offer, and even individual reels or series, clear MAGIC-style names
so the viewer instantly knows who it is for and what they get.

## Execution: Your First Wins
Build ONE high-value, hard-to-compare offer and make it work before chasing anything else. Charge
well, justified by the four value drivers. Add scarcity, urgency, and bonuses; reverse risk with an
honest guarantee; name it for the avatar. Master this one offer before pouring money into ads.
**In a content plan:** point your content at proving and selling that one core offer, not five
half-built ones — a focused set of reels around one promise converts better than scattered ideas.

## Gate checklist (from this book)
Before an offer-related content plan or reel ships, confirm:
1. **Value Equation** — any offer or proof reel visibly RAISES the dream outcome or belief-it-works,
   and LOWERS how much time and effort it looks like (Ch 6).
2. **Sell the dream, not the tool** — content leads with the destination, then shows the method (Ch 9).
3. **One clear, hard-to-compare offer** — not five vague ones; it stands in a category of one (Ch 2, 3).
4. **Speaks to ONE named avatar** — the exact viewer feels "this is for me" (Ch 4).
5. **Scarcity and urgency are REAL** — every limit, count, and deadline is genuine, never fabricated
   (Ch 12, 13).
6. **Bonuses are named and each kills one objection** — real value, not filler (Ch 14).
7. **Guarantees promise process or effort, NEVER a guaranteed income or result** — this is an
   ad-platform hard rule, not a style choice (Ch 15).
8. **Named with MAGIC** — the offer (and key reels) tell the viewer who it's for and what they get,
   with no duration-plus-income claim (Ch 16).
