## Step 8 (CTA) — the ask must never point at plumbing the creator has not built

**Ask per `asking-rules.md`.**

The pieces of a good ask were already scattered across the method: the stage ladder in Step 4,
the plumbing gate in the Rules Gate, the ONE conversion action in Step 7. Nothing joined them
into a per-slot process, so the ask was the row most likely to be improvised.

**Substance conditional (FRFRMU-1032):** on an activation slot, once `step-08-join-recipe.md`'s
Layer 2 read-map has a `prospect_research` objection for this slot, the ask's setup line (the
sentence handling that objection BEFORE the CTA) cites it. This recipe's own class/plumbing
rules below are unchanged and still decide WHETHER an ask runs at all — substance never widens
that gate. Key absent → the ask is chosen exactly as this recipe already specifies.

**The one sentence to remember: an ask you cannot fulfil is worse than no ask at all.** A
viewer who comments the keyword and gets nothing back does not try again. Work CT.1 → CT.5 in
order.

### CT.1 — Stage sets the class of ask

The slot's funnel role and the account's stage (Step 4) decide what KIND of ask is even on the
table. The eleven ask types Reach Machine records are `no_cta`, `follow`, `save`, `share`,
`comment_open`, `comment_keyword`, `dm_open`, `link_in_bio`, `visit_site`, `attend_event` and
`watch_more`.

| Funnel role | Asks that fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `reach` (TOF) | `follow`, `save`, `share`, `comment_open`, `watch_more` | light, costs the viewer nothing, does not need a funnel behind it |
| `nurture` (MOF) | `comment_open`, `save`, `share` — plus `comment_keyword` **only when it is plumbed** | the viewer knows you now, so a small step is fair |
| `activation` (BOF) | **the plan's ONE conversion action, and nothing else** | see below |

**Conditional (p04, FRFRMU-1035):** when `prospect_research` exists, an activation reel
handles the TOP-ranked objection (cited from the card) before the ask — an activation reel is
not finished until the objection it's overcoming is named. No `prospect_research` key → this
sentence is a no-op, unchanged from before.

**Every activation slot in the month points at the SAME conversion action.** The plan already
names one in its goal section (Step 7). If one activation reel sends people to a DM keyword,
another to the bio link and a third to a booking page, the month has three half-built funnels
instead of one working one. This is countable, so the Rules Gate counts it.

**When `offer_stack.bonuses` exists (`step-07-2-offer-stack.md` Part C, FRFRMU-1078):** the
scripted answer to "can you do it cheaper?" in any DM/CTA copy is a bonus, **never a price
cut.** No `bonuses` key → this sentence is a no-op, unchanged from before.

**When `offer_stack.guarantee.signed` is true (Part D, FRFRMU-1078):** the activation ask MAY
cite the guarantee — formula shape only, X a deliverable. Unsigned → no guarantee wording
anywhere in the ask.

**Offer details come from `grand_slam_offer` when it exists (Part F, FRFRMU-1078)** — price,
bonuses, guarantee and deadline all read from that one page, never from a component key
directly. No `grand_slam_offer` → this sentence is a no-op.

**The ladder is a default with an exception, not a law.** A cold-start creator whose automation
is genuinely built and tested may run a keyword ask — say plainly that it will get few replies
at this size, record it as a deliberate decision in the plan, and move on. What is never allowed
is the ask with nothing behind it.

**Pick, don't default (founder decision 2026-09-15, FRFRMU-1544).** A real audit found every
reel in a plan asking the SAME thing ("DM me the word GYM") regardless of its funnel role —
"CTA variety means BOTH the wording AND the ask itself," not wording alone. The table above
lists more than one allowed ask per `reach`/`nurture` row on purpose: pick ACROSS that list,
justified by CT.3's `get_cta_library`/`get_content_breakdown` evidence for what performs in
this niche for this role, not by habit. **At least 3 distinct `cta_type` values across the
whole plan** — `cta_type_variety` warns below that (strictness-ramp default; becomes a
blocker once tuned).

### CT.2 — The plumbing check: the rule this row exists for

Competitor evidence says what WORKS. The creator's own funnel assets, captured at intake, say
what can actually be FULFILLED. The second one wins every time.

| Ask | What must already exist |
|---|---|
| `comment_keyword`, `dm_open` | the lead magnet AND the DM automation, **built AND tested** |
| `link_in_bio`, `visit_site` | the destination page, live and working today |
| `attend_event` | the event, with a real date |
| `follow`, `save`, `share`, `comment_open`, `watch_more` | nothing — these always work |

**"Tested" means somebody actually ran it.** Ask the creator to send the keyword to their own
account from a second phone and screenshot what came back. A "yes it's set up" with no test is
recorded as an assumption in the plan, and the ask is treated as **not plumbed**.

**Not plumbed → step DOWN the ladder, never fake the ask.** A keyword ask with no automation
becomes reply-bait: *"tell me which of the two you'd try first"* instead of *"comment PLAN and
I'll DM it"*. Write the reason into the row so nobody re-upgrades it by accident.

**If the automation is worth building, schedule it — do not silently downgrade forever.** Step
12 explains how: put the automation in the plan's prerequisites with a week, and give the reel a
later week that depends on it. That way the strong ask survives and the order stays honest.

**If a competitor dossier exists (`competitor_dossier__<handle>`, `step-02-8-dossier.md`),
benchmark against it: the destination this reel's CTA points to should be at least as concrete
as the best approved competitor's `landing` line.** No dossier key → this paragraph does
nothing, the row is judged exactly as above.

### CT.3 — Evidence: an ask is judged by RESPONSES, not views

This is the one place where the obvious number is the wrong number.

**Views measure the reel. Comments measure the ask.** A reel with 400k views and eleven comments
had a great hook and a dead CTA. Ranking asks by views tells you which asks happened to ride
popular reels — it tells you nothing about whether anybody did the thing.

**The two reads, both free:**

1. **`get_cta_library`** — the ask patterns themselves. Read `types` for the mix and `examples`
   for the real `cta_text` wording, each with its `username` and `url`. Use it for **wording**,
   and note that its own ranking is by views, so do not rank on it.
2. **`get_content_breakdown`** with the `cta_type` dimension, filtered to this slot's slice:

   ```
   get_content_breakdown(
       filters={"funnel_stage": ["<this slot's role>"], "niche": ["<the niche>"],
                "performance_tag": ["<the winner tags this role maps to>"]},
       dimensions=["cta_type"])
   ```

   Each row carries `count`, `median_comments` and `reliability`.

**Rank the candidates by `count` × account spread × `median_comments`.** Never by `median_views`
and never by a blended number — the ground rules in `playbook/step-08-hook-recipe.md` say why.
Account spread is not on the breakdown row, so count the distinct `username` values in the
matching reels (the funnel-role source table Step 3 rule 5a already built, or a
`get_posts_detailed` read filtered to that `cta_type`). Three accounts making the same ask is a
pattern; one account making it thirty times is one person's habit.

**Honest small numbers apply.** Under about five reels, this is a bet — say the count out loud
and tag the row DATA-INFERRED or JUDGMENT.

### CT.4 — Write the ask

- **ONE primary ask per reel.** A passive `follow` may ride along as a secondary; two active
  asks compete and both lose.
- **Borrow the proven pattern, write their words.** Same rule as the hook: the near-clone test
  in `RULES_GATE.md` Gate 4 applies to CTA wording too.
- **Placement:** spoken at the end, echoed in the caption, on-screen overlay optional. The three
  must say the SAME thing — `playbook/step-08-caption-recipe.md` C.3 is the other half of this
  rule, and a caption asking for a DM on a reel asking for a comment is a contradiction the gate
  catches.
- **Spell an activation ask out in full.** *"DM me the word PLAN and I'll send it over"* beats
  *"link in bio"* because there is nothing left to work out.
- **The month's asks, side by side (FRFRMU-1544).** Before finishing Step 8, table every reel's
  `cta` and `cta_type`. Any opener used more than twice (e.g. "Comment X for…" repeated with
  only the keyword changed) is rewritten from `get_cta_library.examples` wording (`cta_text`,
  its own `username`/`url`) in the creator's voice — the CLASS of ask from CT.1 never changes,
  only the words. `cta_wording_variety` catches this mechanically at save time; this table is
  how you catch it BEFORE the save, not after.

### CT.5 — The done-test, and the receipt this ask must carry

Before the row is finished:

- the ask matches the slot's **stage class** (CT.1);
- for any ask that needs plumbing, the plumbing is **confirmed built AND tested** — or the ask
  was stepped down and the reason is written in the row;
- **every activation row in the plan carries the SAME conversion action**;
- the spoken ask, the caption echo and any on-screen overlay are **identical**;
- **the receipt is attached** when the ask is presented as evidence-backed. Its shape is fixed,
  and the plan validator reads it:

  | field | what goes in it |
  |---|---|
  | `kind` | `cta_pattern` |
  | `cta_type` | the ask type from the eleven above |
  | `count` | how many reels in the slice made this ask — the denominator, a real number above zero |
  | `accounts` | how many DIFFERENT accounts made it |
  | `median` | that row's `median_comments` |
  | `metric_used` | `comments` — an ask's job is responses, so comments is the matching metric |

  A receipt with no count is not a receipt, and a `cta_pattern` receipt quoting views is
  refused: the number has to match the claim. Never invent a field that reads like a score, a
  confidence or a probability — the validator refuses those by name.

### What we can and cannot measure — say it out loud

**We cannot read comment TEXT and we cannot see DMs.** Nothing in Reach Machine tells you what
people wrote back or how many DMs a keyword produced, for a competitor or for the creator. So:

- an ask's result is judged by the **comment COUNT** on the creator's own reels — scope the read
  to their own account (Step 1.6, `usernames=[their handle]`) — plus what the creator reports
  back from their own inbox;
- **never state a conversion we cannot observe.** "This CTA converted 12%" is a claim no tool in
  this product can support. "Your reels with this ask get about four times the comments your
  typical reel gets, from nine reels" is one it can.

That honesty is also the measurement plan for the month: the weekly ritual (Step 8 item 8) tracks
comments on activation reels, and the creator tells you what actually landed in the inbox.
